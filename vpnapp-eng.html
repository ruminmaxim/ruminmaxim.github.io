VPN App

About the project

A VPN client and an accompanying website.

Research for the MVP

Inside the company, we surveyed employees, friends, and acquaintances from different countries:
  • «Do you use VPN, and how often?»
  • «If yes, what do you use it for?»
  • «Which features do you use?»
I no longer have the exact survey results, but the broad picture was clear: among more than 150 respondents, about 90% used VPN in some form. Usage frequency varied, but the most common answer was «rarely, to access a blocked resource.» Other answers showed that most respondents simply clicked Connect. This matched competitor research: in most VPN apps, the main window was very simple, while paid functionality was hidden either behind the main button, meaning no free version or only a trial, or behind additional paid servers.

App design

For this project, I designed the main app screen as the key experience based on the research. I prepared the visual style, sketches, animation, and the final version of the main screen. Further app development and additional screens, such as settings, were handed over to another designer to balance the workload.
An additional advantage of this layout is that it can be adapted to any platform quite easily.

Additional research

Based on the survey, we understood that many users only needed free access to one server that could open a blocked resource. So we ran additional online research, including trends and competitors, to find other user pain points. It helped us solve two tasks:
  1. Focus on users who had specific requirements and feature needs.
  2. Draw users’ attention to features they had not thought about before.

Website design

When designing the website and shaping the content, we focused on the user pains found during the research. We also tried to present the material in a way that would make users consider benefits and additional opportunities beyond the basic VPN use case. For the visual style, we started from the app and prepared colorful illustrations to make the content easier to scan and understand.

Results

The website performed well during targeted ad launch and indexed well because of the amount of text. Heatmap and behavior analytics showed that our hypotheses were right: users read the text blocks, followed internal links, explored the information, and the CTA elements were placed correctly.
During brainstorming, we came up with the idea for a separate independent service for rating and reviewing VPN applications.
