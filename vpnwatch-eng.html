VPNWatch

Background

During a brainstorming session about promoting the internal VPN product, we came up with an idea for a separate independent service with ratings and reviews of VPN applications. The website had to focus on organic traffic and contain a large amount of useful information about VPN products.

Team

The core project team was small: a project manager, an analyst, an SEO consultant, a designer, a small development team, and outsourced copywriters.

MVP launch

First, we decided to release an MVP and avoid spending too much time on design, graphics, and development. It was important to get the key metrics quickly and publish articles right away so the site could start being indexed.

Results

The site indexed well, and the article topics were chosen successfully, but users left pages quite quickly while reading. We ran several A/B tests on existing pages. They showed that pages with more graphics and infographics retained users better and increased page depth.

Website update and version 2.0 launch

  • Design. We updated the design to make it more modern while trying to preserve the existing HTML structure, primarily to avoid harming indexed pages. This time, we approached design more comprehensively: prepared a palette, changed typography, and built a design library. We also developed the brand and visual style for the project to make it more recognizable and useful for social media promotion. This style became consistent across the whole project, mini-services, and graphics.
  • Content. We added a large amount of graphics and infographics to existing articles, created a VPN app rating, and added comparison functionality. We also made many one-page mini-services, often with an entertainment angle, described below.

To grow the website, we created and launched different formats and ideas

I was involved in brainstorming each of these creative formats.
  • Information services
    VPN service statistics over time and comparison tables for VPN clients.
  • Curated landing pages
    Best VPN services for streaming, country-specific landing pages, and similar formats.
  • Useful mini-services
    Search through proxy, regional search, and website security checks.
  • Entertainment pages
    Password security checks and infographic articles.
All of these formats were meant to increase the website’s overall popularity through deeper engagement and social media activity, in addition to SEO articles.
In practice, the project felt like a startup: varied tasks, constant communication, and active brainstorming. The design scope was broad: branding, website and page design, SEO articles, analytics review in Google Stats, preparing many graphics and infographics, creating different mini-services with their own mechanics, and preparing landing pages. I would call this one of my most memorable projects, largely because of the variety of activities and deep involvement.
