UX research for a rebrand

Task

Research the key user flow after the rebrand and collect user feedback on the new visual style.

Challenges

  1. Check whether the rebrand would negatively affect usability.
  2. Collect respondents’ feedback on the new style.
  3. Test both desktop and mobile versions.

Audience

We invited people who had recently joined the project, as well as company employees who were not connected to it. This matched the target audience well, because the main users of the platform are IT specialists looking for work. We tried to include people from different roles: development, business analysis, QA, and others. Using internal resources also helped us complete the research quickly.

Moderated research

First we ran moderated sessions and asked respondents to complete the key action in one of the scenarios. The study was remote, with respondents sharing their screens. We prepared interactive prototypes in two versions: desktop and mobile. Each version was tested separately. The task was: «Try to find a vacancy for yourself and apply for it.» During the session, we asked participants to comment on their actions, thoughts, impressions, doubts, and pain points. We also asked follow-up questions during and at the end of the meeting. In addition to usability testing, we asked respondents about the new design. As a result, we collected extra feedback on the rebrand, and some of it later became the basis for design refinements.
Each test session involved two designers. One talked to the respondent, while the other captured insights. This saved time on transcribing meeting recordings.

Result analysis

We ran 10 testing sessions and grouped the collected insights. The rebrand did not have a negative effect on the user experience: all respondents completed the scenario successfully. We also found several usability improvement opportunities and left them for further exploration. During the observations, we kept refining the prototype based on how users interacted with it. This helped us prepare a stronger and more complete prototype for the following quantitative study.

Quantitative research

After the qualitative study, we ran a quantitative test to validate the findings on a larger respondent sample. For this step, we uploaded the interactive prototype to Maze. Respondents were recruited through an email mailing list. The conclusions from the qualitative research were confirmed.

Results

  • We confirmed that the rebrand did not harm the key user flow: users completed the task successfully, so the rebrand was unlikely to hurt the core metrics after release.
  • The rebrand mostly created a positive impression among respondents.
  • The most frequent visual feedback became the basis for rebrand refinements.
After finishing the research and applying refinements, we moved on to the platform redesign and design system update.
